The job

Keep the week. Do not restart on Monday.

Most hotel “gyms” are a rack of dumbbells and a treadmill. Most programmes assume a rack, a platform, and an unbroken week at home.

If you fly often, or you split the week across two houses, the plan that only works at home A is not a plan. It is a streak you break every ten days.

A hotel kettlebell workout (or hotel-dumbbell swap) is how you finish the week you are already in — the night you land, a short floor mid-stay, the morning before you fly.

What you need

One bell. A small room. That is the list.

  • Preferred one kettlebell you travel with, or keep at home A / home B.
  • Hotel swap a single dumbbell from the hotel rack if that is all there is.
  • Space enough to stand, hinge, and press without hitting the minibar.
  • Quiet option no swings when floors are thin, it is late, or walls are shared.

You do not need a full set, a coach on Zoom, or an app. Training plan, not treatment — if you have an injury or a condition, ask a qualified clinician before you start.

Session shape

Three hotel sessions that still count.

Session 1 is the night you arrive. Session 2 is a short floor that still finishes the week. Session 3 is the morning before you fly.

Each one is warm-up, main work, rest, and a one-line why — written for a small room, with a quiet variant when you cannot swing.

Same session at home with your own bell: swap the tool, do not restart the programme.

Quiet floor

Neighbours first. Ego second.

Carpet, thin floors, late check-in: skip the swings. The quiet version still trains — slower lowers, floor work, presses and carries that do not announce you to the room below.

If the rack is empty, towel + bodyweight can still close the session. The week matters more than the perfect implement.
